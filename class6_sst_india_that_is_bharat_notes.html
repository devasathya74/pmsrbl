Chapter Overview & Core Concepts (अध्याय सारांश)

Historical Geography

English Summary

The chapter "India, That Is Bharat" explores how our motherland has been identified, defined, and named over thousands of years. While modern India is a constitutional republic with established political borders, historically India was perceived as a distinct geographical entity bound by natural barriers—the snowy Himalayas in the north and the expansive ocean in the south.

Over millennia, our ancestors and visiting travelers gave this land various names: Sapta Sindhava (the Rigvedic land of seven rivers), Bharatavarsha (the realm of King Bharata's descendants), and Jambudvipa (the island of the rose-apple tree). Foreigners transformed the river name Sindhu into Hind, Indos, and eventually India. Article 1 of our Constitution celebrates this civilizational continuity with the phrase: "India, that is Bharat".

हिंदी सारांश (सरल भाषा में)

यह अध्याय "भारत, जो कि इंडिया है" हमें सिखाता है कि हमारे देश को प्राचीन काल से लेकर आज तक किन-किन नामों से पुकारा गया और इसकी भौगोलिक पहचान कैसे बनी। आधुनिक भारत एक संविधान-सम्मत लोकतांत्रिक देश है, लेकिन प्राचीन काल में इसे प्राकृतिक सीमाओं—उत्तर में हिमालय और दक्षिण में हिन्द महासागर—से घिरे एक संपूर्ण सांस्कृतिक व भौगोलिक क्षेत्र के रूप में देखा जाता था।

ऋग्वेद में पश्चिमोत्तर क्षेत्र को सप्त सिन्धवः (सात नदियों की भूमि) कहा गया। बाद में महाकाव्यों और पुराणों में पूरे उपमहाद्वीप को भारतवर्ष और जम्बूद्वीप नाम दिया गया। विदेशी यात्रियों (ईरानी, यूनानी, चीनी) ने सिन्धु नदी के आधार पर हिंद, इन्दोई और इंडिया नाम दिए। भारतीय संविधान का अनुच्छेद 1 इस ऐतिहासिक निरंतरता को संजोते हुए उद्घोष करता है: "इंडिया, अर्थात् भारत"।

The Big Questions (पृष्ठ 75 के मुख्य प्रश्न व समाधान)

Core Curriculum
Q1

How do we define India?

(हम भारत को कैसे परिभाषित करते हैं?)

Answer / उत्तर:

Today, India is defined as a modern nation-state with constitutional borders, 28 states, 8 Union Territories, and a distinct population governed by the Constitution of India.

Historically, however, while political boundaries and kingdoms shifted constantly, Indians defined their homeland as a magnificent geographical and cultural entity bounded naturally by the snow-capped Himalayas in the north and the great ocean in the south. Ancient texts like the Vishnu Purana and Tamil Sangam literature celebrate this continuous landmass where people shared common civilizational roots, values, and traditions.

(हिंदी व्याख्या: आज भारत को निश्चित सीमाओं, 28 राज्यों और 8 केंद्र शासित प्रदेशों वाले एक आधुनिक संवैधानिक राष्ट्र-राज्य के रूप में परिभाषित किया जाता है। ऐतिहासिक रूप से, भारत की पहचान इसकी प्राकृतिक भूगोल—उत्तर में हिमालय और दक्षिण में समुद्र—तथा हजारों वर्षों से चली आ रही सांस्कृतिक एकता से परिभाषित होती है।)

Q2

What were the ancient names for India?

(भारत के प्राचीन नाम कौन-कौन से थे?)

Answer / उत्तर:

Throughout history, India was known by multiple names given both by its own inhabitants and by foreign travelers:

Names Given by Inhabitants (भारतीयों द्वारा):

  • Sapta Sindhava: Land of seven rivers (Rig Veda).
  • Bharatavarsha: Land of the Bharatas (Mahabharata, Puranas).
  • Jambudvipa: Island of the Jamun tree (used by Emperor Ashoka).
  • Aryavarta: Land of the noble ones (Northern plains).

Names Given by Foreigners (विदेशी यात्रियों द्वारा):

  • Hind / Hindu: Persians (from river Sindhu).
  • Indoi / Indike: Ancient Greeks (from river Indus).
  • Yintu / Tianzhu: Ancient Chinese travelers (Xuanzang).
  • Hindustan: Medieval Persian & Islamic records.
  • India: Latin and modern European languages.

Civilizational Definition: Vishnu Purana & Article 1

Sacred Shloka & Constitution
Vishnu Purana (Book II, Chapter 3, Verse 1)

उत्तरं यत्समुद्रस्य हिमाद्रेश्चैव दक्षिणम्।
वर्षं तद्भारतं नाम भारती यत्र संततिः॥

"Uttaraṃ yatsamudrasya himādreścaiva dakṣiṇam |
Varṣaṃ tadbhārataṃ nāma bhāratī yatra santatiḥ ||"

English Translation:

"The country that lies north of the ocean and south of the snowy mountains is called Bhārata; there dwell the children and descendants of Bharata."

सरल हिंदी भावार्थ:

"जो देश समुद्र (हिन्द महासागर) के उत्तर में और बर्फीले पर्वत (हिमालय) के दक्षिण में स्थित है, उसका नाम भारत है और वहाँ रहने वाले लोग भरत की संतान हैं।"

The Constitution of India · Article 1(1)

"India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States."

(इंडिया, अर्थात् भारत, राज्यों का एक संघ होगा।)

Constitutional Significance: The framers of our Constitution united our 5,000-year-old civilizational identity (Bharat) with our globally recognized modern democratic republic (India).

State Whether True or False (सत्य या असत्य)

Complete Textbook Exercise

(a) The Rig Veda describes the entire geography of India.

FALSE

Reason: The Rig Veda only describes the northwestern region of the Indian subcontinent, which it calls Sapta Sindhava (the land of seven rivers). It does not describe the central, southern, or eastern regions.

(b) The Vishnu Purana describes the entire Subcontinent.

TRUE

Reason: The Vishnu Purana explicitly delineates the entire subcontinent as the territory lying north of the ocean and south of the snowy Himalayan mountains under the name Bhārata.

(c) In Ashoka's time, 'Jambudvipa' included what is today India, parts of Afghanistan, Bangladesh and Pakistan.

TRUE

Reason: Emperor Ashoka's rock edicts discovered in present-day Afghanistan, Pakistan, India, and Bangladesh use the name Jambudvipa to refer to this vast shared realm under his rule.

(d) The Mahabharata lists many regions, including Kashmir, Kutch, and Kerala.

TRUE

Reason: The epic Mahabharata (particularly in the Bhishma Parva) provides a vast geographical gazetteer listing hundreds of regions (Janapadas), including Kashmira in the north, Kaccha (Kutch) in the west, and Kerala in the deep south.

(e) The term 'Hindustān' first appeared in a Greek inscription more than 2,000 years ago.

FALSE

Reason: The term Hindustān first appeared in an ancient Persian Sasanian inscription (by King Shapur I at Naqsh-e Rustam) around 262 CE, not in a Greek inscription.

(f) In ancient Persian, the word 'Hindu' refers to the Hindu religion.

FALSE

Reason: In ancient Old Persian, 'Hindu' was purely a geographical term referring to the river Sindhu and the land/inhabitants surrounding it. It had no religious connotation at that time.

(g) 'Bharata' is a name given to India by foreign travellers.

FALSE

Reason: Bhārata is a native, indigenous Indian name rooted in Vedic texts (the Bharata tribe in the Rig Veda) and the Puranas (descendants of King Bharata).

Multiple Choice Questions (बहुविकल्पीय प्रश्न)

Exam-Oriented MCQs

1. What does the term 'Sapta Sindhava' literally refer to?

(a) Land of seven cities
(b) Land of seven rivers
(c) Land of seven mountains
(d) Land of seven oceans
Correct Answer: (b) Land of seven rivers — It refers to River Sindhu and its six tributaries mentioned in the Rig Veda.

2. Which name was used by Emperor Ashoka in his edicts to describe the subcontinent?

(a) Hindustan
(b) Aryavarta
(c) Jambudvipa
(d) Indike
Correct Answer: (c) Jambudvipa — Ashoka used 'Jambudvipa' across his inscriptions to denote his realm.

3. According to the Vishnu Purana, Bharat is the land situated:

(a) North of the snowy mountains and south of the ocean
(b) North of the ocean and south of the snowy mountains
(c) East of the Indus and west of the Ganga
(d) South of the equator
Correct Answer: (b) North of the ocean and south of the snowy mountains (उत्तरं यत्समुद्रस्य हिमाद्रेश्चैव दक्षिणम्).

4. The names 'Yintu' and 'Tianzhu' were used for India by ancient travelers from:

(a) Greece
(b) Persia
(c) China
(d) Rome
Correct Answer: (c) China — Chinese pilgrims like Xuanzang referred to India as Yintu/Tianzhu.

5. In the Indian Constitution, the official definition of our nation in Article 1 is:

(a) Bharat, that is Hindustan
(b) India, that is Bharat
(c) Hindustan, that is Aryavarta
(d) Jambudvipa, that is India
Correct Answer: (b) India, that is Bharat — Recognizing both the civilizational and modern constitutional names.

Descriptive Questions & Answers (लघु एवं दीर्घ उत्तरीय प्रश्न)

Full Solutions

A Short Answer Questions (लघु उत्तरीय प्रश्न):

Q1. Why was the northwestern region of ancient India called 'Sapta Sindhava'? Name the seven rivers.

In the Rig Veda, the northwest subcontinent was called Sapta Sindhava (the land of seven rivers) because it was nourished by the mighty Sindhu (Indus) and six other major rivers:

  1. Sindhu (Indus)
  2. Sarasvati (sacred Vedic river)
  3. Vitasta (modern Jhelum)
  4. Asikni (modern Chenab)
  5. Parushni (modern Ravi)
  6. Vipash (modern Beas)
  7. Shutudri (modern Sutlej)

Q2. How did the word 'Hindu' originate, and did it originally refer to a religion?

The word 'Hindu' originated as a geographical term, not a religious one. Ancient Persians who traded with and encountered the Indian subcontinent referred to the River Sindhu. Because Old Persian sound rules often replaced the Sanskrit initial 'S' sound with 'H', Sindhu was pronounced as Hind or Hindu. It simply referred to the land and people living along or beyond the Indus river.

Q3. What does ancient Tamil Sangam literature say about the geographical extent of India?

Ancient Tamil Sangam texts (such as the Purananuru and Silappadikaram) define the sacred subcontinent as extending "from Kumari (Kanyakumari) in the south to the Imayam (Himalayas) in the north" (Imayam mudal Kumari varai). This proves that people across the far south had a clear mental map of the entire subcontinent as one single cultural entity.

B Long Answer Questions (दीर्घ उत्तरीय प्रश्न):

Q1. In the course of its history, India has been called by many names by its inhabitants. Explain this statement in detail.

Over thousands of years, the inhabitants of India developed several names for their homeland based on geography, rulers, and cultural identity:

  • 1. Sapta Sindhava (Rigvedic Period): The earliest name found in the Rig Veda, describing the fertile northwest region sustained by the River Indus and its tributaries.
  • 2. Bharatavarsha (Epic & Puranic Period): Derived from the ancient Vedic tribe Bharata and King Bharata (son of Dushyanta and Shakuntala). The Mahabharata and Vishnu Purana used Bharatavarsha to denote the entire subcontinent stretching between the Himalayas and the oceans.
  • 3. Jambudvipa (Epics & Buddhist Texts): Literally translating to "the island of the rose-apple (jamun) tree," this term was used in cosmological texts and formally inscribed on the stone rock edicts of Emperor Ashoka to designate his empire.
  • 4. Aryavarta: Found in texts like the Manusmriti, meaning "the land of the noble ones," referring to the vast northern plains between the Himalayas and the Vindhyas.

Q2. Explain the significance of the phrase "India, that is Bharat" in Article 1 of the Constitution of India.

When the Constituent Assembly debated the naming of the nation in September 1949, they chose the historic formulation: "India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States." This phrase carries deep civilizational and legal significance:

  1. Bridge Between Heritage and Modernity: Bharat honors the 5,000-year-old cultural, spiritual, and literary heritage of the subcontinent, while India reflects the modern sovereign republic recognized across international forums and treaties.
  2. Equal Legal Standing: The Constitution recognizes both names on an equal footing. Official government documents and currency notes use both: "Government of India" / "भारत सरकार".
  3. Unity in Diversity: It satisfies the sentiments of all linguistic and cultural communities, celebrating our roots without discarding our modern global identity.
