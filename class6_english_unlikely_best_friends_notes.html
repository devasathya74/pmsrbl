The Story: The Unlikely Best Friends (मूल कहानी एवं सारांश)

By Subba Rao
Scene 1: The Lonely Royal Elephant in the Palace Stables

Gajaraj, the elephant, lived in the best booth of the royal stables. The king was very fond of Gajaraj, and he had ordered that the elephant should be well looked after. In spite of royal comforts, Gajaraj was sad because he had no friends. The mahout, or elephant trainer, was the only one he ever interacted with. The mahout was a kind man who served Gajaraj food and gave him a bath in the elephant pond daily. He was a good caretaker, but not a friend. Gajaraj often looked out of his stable booth, wishing: “I wish I had a friend I can play with.”

Scene 2: The Arrival of a Hungry Visitor

One late evening, a stray dog wandered into the stable. Gajaraj could see that the visitor was tired, thin, and hungry. Instead of chasing the stranger away, Gajaraj pushed some of the food he was munching towards the dog. The visitor wagged his tail, looked up at the giant elephant to convey his thanks, and then turned his full attention to the food. He ate greedily until his belly was full. That night, the little dog curled up and slept comfortably right in Gajaraj’s stable.

Scene 3: An Inseparable Friendship Blossoms

From that night, an unlikely friendship grew between the giant elephant and the small dog. Gajaraj named his companion Buntee. The two spent all their time together: they shared their meals, walked together to the elephant pond, and played happily. While bathing, Gajaraj sucked up water into his long trunk and sprayed gentle showers over Buntee. The dog yelped playfully because he hated cold showers, but he loved the game. Gajaraj would then gently lift Buntee in his trunk and place him on his broad head and back, taking him on grand rides around the stable yard.

Scene 4: The Separation & Mutual Grief

One morning, a farmer from a nearby village visited the stables and instantly recognized Buntee as his lost pet dog. Overjoyed to find his missing animal, the farmer tied a rope around Buntee's neck and led him away to his farm. As soon as Buntee was gone, Gajaraj fell into deep despair. He stood motionless in his booth, tears streaming down his face, and completely refused to eat or bathe. Meanwhile, at the farm, Buntee whined continuously, rejected his food bowl, and desperately pulled against the rope to escape back to his elephant friend.

Scene 5: The Empathetic Farmer & Joyous Reunion

Seeing Buntee’s sorrow and untouched food, the farmer’s heart melted. Remembering the radiant joy on Buntee’s face while sitting on Gajaraj's back, he said softly: “Are you missing your friend? Go to him!” and untied the rope. Buntee bolted out of the farm gate and raced straight to the royal stables. Seeing Buntee run into the booth, Gajaraj trumpeted in ecstasy, scooped him up in his trunk, and swung him in the air. The farmer, who had followed the dog, reached the stables. Witnessing the magical reunion, the farmer and the mahout shared a warm hug and agreed to let the two companions stay together forever!

English Summary

The story celebrates the tender, unconditional bond between Gajaraj (a royal elephant) and Buntee (a stray dog). Despite royal luxury, Gajaraj is sad without companionship. A simple act of sharing food unites the giant and the tiny dog into best friends. When Buntee is reclaimed by his farmer owner, both animals plunge into grief and stop eating. Empathy triumphs when the farmer frees Buntee, proving that true friendship recognizes neither differences of size nor boundaries of species.

Critical Reflection: Caretaker vs. Friend (देखभालकर्ता बनाम मित्र)

Core Textbook Question

"The mahout was a good caretaker, but not a friend. What is the difference between a caretaker and a friend?"

A Caretaker (देखभालकर्ता)
  • Fulfills Physical Needs: Provides food, bath, clean bedding, and shelter.
  • Duty-Bound: Performs work as a task, job, or professional responsibility.
  • Interchangeable: Another trained person can perform the exact same tasks.
  • Example: The mahout who fed Gajaraj daily and led him to the pond.
A Friend (सच्चा मित्र)
  • Fulfills Emotional Needs: Provides playfulness, mutual affection, laughter, and belonging.
  • Heart-to-Heart Bond: Created naturally out of love and voluntary choice, not payment.
  • Irreplaceable: Cannot be substituted by any luxury or other person.
  • Example: Buntee, with whom Gajaraj played, cuddled, and shared his soul.

State Whether True or False (सत्य या असत्य)

Detailed Justifications

1. Gajaraj stopped eating when Buntee was taken away by the farmer.

TRUE

Reason: Gajaraj was overwhelmed with grief after losing his companion and completely refused food, standing quietly with tears in his eyes.

2. The mahout understood why Gajaraj had suddenly fallen sad and sick.

TRUE

Reason: The mahout noticed that Gajaraj's health deteriorated precisely after the little dog was taken away, realizing it was heartbreak, not physical illness.

3. Buntee loved taking cold water showers in the pond.

FALSE

Reason: The text notes that Buntee playfully yelped because he hated cold showers, yet he enjoyed playing with Gajaraj.

4. The elephant splashed water on his dog friend using his mouth.

FALSE

Reason: Gajaraj sucked up water and created gentle shower sprays using his long trunk, not his mouth.

5. The farmer was a cruel man who tortured the dog.

FALSE

Reason: The farmer was Buntee's loving original master; when he saw Buntee's tears, he selflessly freed the dog so he could live happily with Gajaraj.

Questions & Answers (लघु एवं दीर्घ उत्तरीय प्रश्न)

Complete Solutions

A Short Answer Questions (लघु उत्तरीय प्रश्न):

Q1. Describe the fun activities Gajaraj and Buntee enjoyed doing together.

Gajaraj and Buntee spent joyful days together:

  • They ate meals side-by-side, sharing sweet fruits and green fodder.
  • They walked together to the elephant pond, where Gajaraj sprayed showers on Buntee with his trunk.
  • Gajaraj gently lifted Buntee with his trunk onto his back for rides.
  • They slept close to each other inside the warm stable booth every night.

Q2. Why did the farmer hug Buntee when he saw him in the royal stable?

The farmer hugged Buntee out of sheer joy and relief. Buntee was his beloved pet who had gone missing days ago. Finding him safe filled his heart with happiness.

Q3. How did both Gajaraj and Buntee react when they were forcefully separated?

Separation plunged both animals into profound depression. Gajaraj refused all food, stopped visiting the pond for baths, and stood weeping silently. Meanwhile, at the farm, Buntee kept whining, refused his bowl of food, and strained against his rope, proving that animal friendship is rooted in pure love.

B Long Answer Questions (दीर्घ उत्तरीय प्रश्न):

Q1. Discuss the character of the farmer. Why is his decision to release Buntee commendable?

The farmer is portrayed as a kind, sensitive, and deeply empathetic human being:

  • Sensitive & Caring: He noticed the tears in Buntee's eyes and remembered the dog's joyful face when sitting on the elephant's back.
  • Selfless Love: Rather than forcing the dog to stay with him in misery, he prioritized Buntee's emotional well-being over his own ownership.
  • Friendly Spirit: When he followed Buntee to the stables and saw the magnificent joy of the reunion, he befriended the mahout and agreed to let them remain together.

Q2. What important life lessons about friendship and kindness do we learn from Subba Rao's story?

"The Unlikely Best Friends" offers several timeless moral lessons:

  1. Kindness Connects Hearts: Gajaraj's simple gesture of sharing food initiated a lifelong friendship.
  2. Luxury Cannot Replace Companionship: Royal status is hollow without loved ones to share life with.
  3. Differences Do Not Matter: A huge elephant and a small dog became soulmates because affection resides in the heart.
  4. Empathy Creates Harmony: The farmer and mahout respected animal feelings, creating peace and happiness for all.

Grammar Focus: The Past Tense (भूतकाल के रूप)

Poorvi Grammar Unit

The story uses different forms of the Past Tense to describe completed, ongoing, or earlier actions in the past:

1. Simple Past Tense Subject + V2 (Past Form)

Used for completed past actions.

"The elephant picked up the dog."
"The farmer untied the rope."
2. Past Continuous Tense was / were + Verb-ing

Used for actions ongoing in the past.

"The dog was watching the elephant."
"Gajaraj was munching on food."
3. Past Perfect Tense had + V3 (Past Participle)

Used for actions completed before another past event.

"The farmer, who had followed the dog, arrived."
"The dog had strayed from his home."

Textbook Passage Completion Exercise: Choose the Correct Verb Form

When Buntee (came back / was coming back), the mahout (noticed / had noticed) that Gajaraj immediately lifted his trunk. Both animals (felt / had felt) immense joy because they (missed / had missed) each other deeply during the separation.

Solved Answers: 1. came back · 2. noticed · 3. felt · 4. had missed.
